(6) Stocks of Wine, etc. The stock of wine, spirits and beer allowed on board shall be limited strictly to the requirements of each mess. Before any of these articles is ordered by a gunroom mess, or obtained by them from other messes, a list of what is required, signed by the wine secretary shall be taken, together with the wine book, showing what has already been received  or  ordered  by  the  mess,  to  the  Captain  for  his  approval  and  signature,  and  these permits shall afterwards be pasted into the wine book.


(7) Supplies  of  Wine  etc. The  Captain  may  sanction  supplies  of  wine,  etc.  being received  from  messes  at  the  risk  of  the  wine  merchant,  provided  it  is  to  be  paid  form,  as consumed by at least quarterly installments

- Payments  of  Mess  Bills. (1)  The  Captain  shall  see  that  all  mess  and  wine  bills  are  settled monthly by each member. Payments must be made in cash or by cheques. A part from such payments  by  cheque,  private  bills  or  cheques  shall  not  be  received  by  or  cashed  from  the mess,  wine  or  other  funds.  Any  pay,  half  pay,  pension,  retired  pay,  gratuity  or  other allowances payable to an officer are liable to be abated in order to meet mess bills or other liabilities in respect of his mess, or any similar debts or liabilities

(2) Mess Bills of Officers under Arrest . Mess Bills of officers under arrest, when they  are  not  able  to  meet  such  bills  from  their  own  resources,  may  be  claimed  from  CDA (Navy). For the procedure which the Captain shall follow when the amount has to be claimed from CDA (Navy). See Financial Regulations.


(3) Debts on Transfer to Another Ship. All officers should clear their bills before proceeding on transfer. If an officer is unable to do so, the Commanding officer shall after due investigation,  direct  the  Supply  Officer-in-Charge,  Naval  Pay  Officer  to  recover  the  said amount from his individual Pay Account under intimation to the officer concerned. A cheque for  the  amount  due  to  the  mess  shall  be  forwarded  by  the  Naval  Pay  Officer  to  the Commanding officer to settle the mess dues. See section 28 (7) of the Navy Act, 1957 and SRO 355 dated 18 Dec 63 as amended by SRO 80 dated 14 Mar 73.


(4) The balance of pay due to a deserter shall not be used to reduce mess debts. Such balance  of  pay  becomes  the  property  of  the  State  and  cannot  be  appropriated  for  the liquidation of mess debts. If, in exceptional circumstances, it should be considered desirable to recover a mess debt from the proceeds of the sale of a deserter's effects prior sanction must be obtained from the Government.


2719. Wine Book. (1)  A  wine  book  (IN  435)  shall  be  kept  and  with  the quarterly mess statement (IN 341) shall be produced whenever called for by competent authority.


(2) Inspection  of  Wine  Books. The  Captain  shall  inspect  the  wine  books  of  the wardroom and gunroom messes weekly; but all officers, wine books and wine chit books shall be examined by him as often as he thinks fit. After each inspection of the books, the Captain shall initial them to show that he has examined them.


(3) When a ship ism inspected, a statement of the condition of the wine fund shall produced, with the wine and gangway books, to enable the inspecting officer to ascertain that the regulations relating to the wine accounts have been complied with.


(4) Balances. If  a  surplus  over  the  amount  of  balance  which  is  necessary  for  the efficient working of the wine fund has accumulated, it may be divided amongst the members in proportion to the amount of their individual interests or for any other legitimate purpose as decided by common consent. Distribution of this surplus may be made on such occasions as are approved by the Mess Committee.


2720. Mess and Wine Debts. (1) Before leaving a port, all debts for goods not at the risk  of  the  merchant  should  be  discharged  if  practicable.  The  Captain  may  approve  of  a reasonable amount of debt being left outstanding on receiving from the mess and undertaking to  pay  it  off  at  the  first  reasonable  opportunity,  which  undertaking  the  Captain  shall  see carried  out.  In  such  cases  it  must  be  understood  that  the  Government  whatever  the Circumstances, will not be responsible for, nor contribute to satisfy, any claim which may be made for losses  which may  occur; and officers, for the protection of their interests,  should make their own arrangements with the tradesmen. The Government will not be liable in any case for the loss of mess stock which has been taken on board.
